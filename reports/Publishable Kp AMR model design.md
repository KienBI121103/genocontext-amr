# Mechanism features beat architecture for Klebsiella AMR

The publishable model is **not a better classifier — it is a better feature space plus a harder evaluation**. On random train/test splits, genome-based *K. pneumoniae* AMR prediction is already saturated: your own GenoContext v1 fusion hits **mean AUROC 0.938 across 17 antibiotics**, AMR-GNN reports **ceftriaxone AUROC 0.985** on 7,072 BV-BRC genomes ([Nat Commun 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69934-8)), and PanKA's LightGBM pipeline trains in 25 CPU-minutes ([iScience 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11369404/)). There is no reviewer-credible headroom left on that axis, and chasing it is how papers get rejected in 2026. The headroom is in three places the entire field has left open: **(1)** annotation-derived *gene-integrity* features — copy number, IS interruption, truncation, allele identity, and FAA-resolved residues — which capture the loss-of-function and dosage mechanisms that gene presence/absence structurally cannot see; **(2)** a **lineage-blocked, 17-drug, per-antibiotic Kp benchmark**, which does not exist and which the field's most-cited 2025 critique explicitly demanded ([PLOS Biol e3003539](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003539)); and **(3)** **low-data-drug rescue** through a factorised gene×drug model, benchmarked against the two published nulls that reviewers will quote at you (p = 0.374 and p = 0.48). On the protocol question: merging train and validation into an 80/20 split buys roughly **ΔAUROC 0.002–0.01**, which is inside cross-validation noise, and costs you principled hyperparameter selection — it is the wrong lever. Spend that budget on repeated nested CV with a *resampled* test set instead. The honest expected outcome is that your model loses to nobody on random splits, wins decisively on lineage-blocked splits and low-data drugs, and produces the first mechanism-level attribution audit in bacterial AMR ML.

## Random-split AUROCs of 0.93–0.99 are measuring lineage, not resistance

The single most important number in your local evidence is not an AUROC — it is that **84–92% of test isolates share a sequence type with training isolates**, and that **8 BioSamples carry two genome IDs each**, with up to five such pairs straddling the split per drug. That is textbook leakage, and it explains why GenoContext v1's fusion model beats RF in only **10 of 17 antibiotics** by paired bootstrap, and why real graph edges beat shuffled edges in only **2 of 17** (aztreonam and TMP-SMX). A model that wins on 2/17 drugs when its central inductive bias is switched off is not learning what its architecture claims.

Yu, Wheeler and Barquist settled the general case with **over 24,000 genomes across five WHO priority pathogens including *K. pneumoniae*, 27 antibiotics and 6,740 trained LightGBM models**, and found that when resistance is confounded with phylogeny, models **"conflate lineage markers with genuine indicators of AMR"** and that **increasing training sample size fails to rescue performance** ([PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)). Their smoking gun is feature instability: for ciprofloxacin, **`parC` ranked highly in most *E. coli* and *S. aureus* clades while `gyrA` appeared in only one *S. aureus* clade** — a canonical, mechanistically certain determinant that the model failed to recover consistently across lineages. They also report that **Gram-negative species gave lower AUC overall**, which they attribute to the outer membrane, and their Kp-specific robustness check (**1,429 of 3,205 genomes, three clades, ciprofloxacin, unitig features**) showed that **changing the feature representation does not fix non-independence**. That last result pre-empts the obvious defence that better features alone solve the problem.

The quantitative cost of honest splitting is already published. Across **78 species–antibiotic datasets (11 species, 44 antimicrobials, 31,195 PATRIC genomes)**, the fraction achieving F1-macro ≥ 0.9 fell from **64% under random folds to 33% under phylogeny-aware folds to 25% under homology-aware folds**; ML beat ResFinder in **66% → 47% → 40%** of cases, and under the two harder regimes **rule-based ResFinder was the single best method in 44% and 50%** of species–drug combinations ([Brief Bioinform bbae206](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/25/3/bbae206/7665136)). *K. pneumoniae* was **not** among the robustly predictable species, its **median F1-macro was 0.79–0.89 under random splits**, and the **worst dataset in the entire study was aztreonam–*K. pneumoniae* at F1-macro 0.59**. Two independent groups that did block by lineage on Kp converge on the same place: ST-grouped `StratifiedGroupKFold` over **3,702 isolates gave ROC-AUC ≈ 0.81** for ertapenem ([repository, self-reported](https://github.com/AMorales22T/AMT-Klebsiella-ertapenem)), and a clade-blocked meropenem pipeline on **554 deduplicated genomes gave AUROC 0.867 internally and 0.81 externally** while its random-split counterpart **collapsed to 22% recall** on external data ([Bioinform Adv vbag193](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformaticsadvances/article/6/1/vbag193/8746877)).

The strategic consequence is blunt. **A lineage-blocked carbapenem AUROC much above 0.85 in your hands should trigger a leakage audit, not a celebration**, and any random-split number above ~0.95 will be read by reviewers as evidence of leakage rather than of a better model. Report both and treat the gap as your headline measurement — no one has published that gap for Kp across 17 drugs, and the critique paper asked for exactly it.

## Bakta annotation already encodes the mechanisms presence/absence cannot see

This is where the genuine modelling novelty lives, and it is CPU-cheap. A large fraction of clinically important Kp resistance is not gene gain — it is gene **loss, truncation, interruption, dosage and allele identity**, and all of those leave signatures in GFF3 coordinates, pseudogene flags, IS cross-references and protein length rather than in gene names. Four mechanism classes are invisible to a presence/absence matrix:

| Mechanism class | Why presence/absence fails | Annotation-derived feature | Strongest cited effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss of function | Gene is near-universal in the species, so the column is constant | Absence, pseudogene flag, CDS/protein length z-score vs cluster-typical length, IS-CDS inside the locus | `ompK36` Y325stop: complementation cut ertapenem MIC **>256-fold** ([PMC12691662](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12691662/)); `ompK35`+`ompK36` double deletion raised meropenem **8-fold** and cefepime **16-fold** ([AAC aac.01275-10](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.01275-10)) |
| Dosage | Gene present in both R and S isolates; only copy number differs | Count of duplicate CDS matching the same determinant across contigs | **`bla_SHV` copy number correlated with TZP, AMC and CAZ MICs** in 10/14 isolates ([AAC aac.01682-25](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/aac.01682-25)); IS26 amplification gave a **52-fold `bla_KPC-2` copy increase** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2165049722016225)); two `bla_KPC-2` copies **quadrupled** the ceftazidime–avibactam MIC ([Spectrum 00331-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/spectrum.00331-24)) |
| Allele identity | Gene family name is shared across functionally different variants | AMRFinderPlus allele suffix in `gene`; FAA residue extraction | `aac(6')-Ib-cr` differs from `aac(6')-Ib` by two residues with a different drug spectrum, and is **62.5–95% prevalent among resistant Kp** depending on cohort ([BMC Microbiol 03383-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12866-024-03383-5)) |
| Context | Same gene, different expression depending on flanking sequence | IS/transposase CDS coordinates relative to the target gene; contig length as a weak plasmid proxy | ISKpn18 in `ramR` raised tigecycline MIC **4→16 mg/L**, and plasmid-borne `ramR` complementation restored susceptibility ([Spectrum 03204-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03204-24)) |

Two refinements make this a model rather than a feature dump. First, **disruption is the signal; missense is not**. The same tigecycline work reports that **I141T and A19V in RamR and R13W in AcrR have no effect on RamA overexpression** ([Spectrum 03204-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03204-24)). An IS-interruption plus pseudogene-flag feature therefore carries *built-in specificity* that a SNP feature lacks — it fires on the causal class and stays silent on the neutral one. `mgrB` is the ideal positive control for validating the detector, because cloning ISKpn26, ISKpn14 and IS903B into a susceptible isolate **demonstrably raised colistin resistance frequency** ([PMC8796155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8796155/)), so the ground truth is unusually firm even though colistin sits outside your 17-drug panel.

Second, **the mechanisms are explicitly epistatic, and that is the argument for pairwise interaction terms rather than an additive model**. Porin loss alone is insufficient: **increased ESBL or AmpC production is a prerequisite for carbapenem resistance, since porin loss alone leaves MICs within the susceptible range** ([ScienceDirect S0924857918300980](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924857918300980)). Nitrofurantoin needs an AND: **separate inactivating mutations in both `nfsA` and `nfsB` are typically required for clinical resistance** ([npj Antimicrob Resist](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44259-024-00056-1)). Fluoroquinolone resistance escalates on co-occurrence: **PMQR and QRDR determinants co-exist in 68.5% (61/89) of isolates and together raise the resistance level** ([BMC Microbiol 02238-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12866-021-02238-7)). And the OmpK36 **L3-loop Gly115-Asp116 insertion gives a 16-fold meropenem MIC increase specifically in the presence of KPC-2**, with crystallographic evidence of pore constriction ([Nat Commun 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-11756-y)). Five interaction terms — carbapenemase×porin-loss, ESBL/AmpC×porin-loss, `nfsA`-LOF×`nfsB`-LOF, `ramR`-disruption×`acrR`-disruption, QRDR×PMQR — are each independently evidenced and each unrepresentable in a purely additive model.

Two cautions. The L3 GD insertion needs the FAA, not the GFF3 — a two-residue insertion shifts coordinates by 6 bp, inside normal length variation — and it is **ST258-enriched, as is `bla_KPC`, while ST258 is 612 of your 3,949 isolates**. Any importance attributed to either must be demonstrated *within* ST258 as well as across lineages, or it is indistinguishable from an ST258 indicator. And `oqxAB` is chromosomally intrinsic in this species, detected in **100% of isolates** in one Iranian series ([PMC11256406](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11256406/)), so its presence/absence column carries zero information while its copy number and `oqxR` status do. One engineering prerequisite: Bakta's README documents AMRFinderPlus-based AMR annotation, ISfinder-derived pre-annotation, UniRef100/90/50 assignment and a pseudogene feature type with a `--skip-pseudo` option ([github.com/oschwengers/bakta](https://github.com/oschwengers/bakta)), but **it does not document the GFF3 attribute schema** and the canonical readthedocs URL returns 404 — so the `IS:<name>` Dbxref convention and pseudogene representation must be confirmed against a real Bakta 1.9.4 output before the extractor depends on them. Your local parser at `/home/kiennm/AMR/AMR_GNN/GenoContext-AMR/src/annotation/bakta.py` currently extracts `gene`, `product`, `Dbxref` (COG/EC) and `contig_length`, and does **not** yet parse UniRef, IS Dbxrefs or pseudogene flags — those three fields are the dependency for everything above.

## Knowledge layers buy interpretation; factorisation buys the low-data drugs

The prior art is unambiguous that a biologically structured architecture will *not*, by itself, raise your AUROC, and you should design around that rather than against it. **KG-TRACE** built exactly the gene→mechanism→drug knowledge graph you would want, on *M. tuberculosis* CRyPTIC with 37,761 isolates, and reported isoniazid **AUROC 0.9760 — identical to XGBoost's 0.9760 and below RF's 0.9806** ([arXiv 2606.26179, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26179)). **P-NET** reached test AUC 0.93 on prostate cancer but **won significantly only below roughly 500 samples** ([Nature 598:348](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03922-4)). A simulation and empirical study of biologically informed networks concludes they mainly exploit linear signal and help interpretability more than accuracy ([PMC12642320](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12642320/)). **GL-HAT**'s learned per-antibiotic attention query lost to plain mean pooling (**AUROC 0.931 vs 0.953**, with XGBoost at 0.950) across 53 antibiotics ([bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.22.695895.full.pdf)) — drug-conditioned attention alone is a weak novelty claim and an empirically losing one.

Multi-task sharing has the same problem. Aytan-Aktug et al. compared per-antibiotic against aggregated multi-output models and found **no significant difference, P = 0.374 for both random forests and neural networks** ([mSystems](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/msystems.00774-19)); the independent benchmark re-ran it and got **P = 0.48**, while cross-species sharing was significantly *harmful* at **P = 1e-13 to 1e-19** ([bbae206](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/25/3/bbae206/7665136)). Drug-as-input is also not new for Kp — Nguyen et al. trained a single XGBoost over **1,668 Kp genomes and 20 antibiotics with the drug merged into the feature vector** back in 2018 ([Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-18972-w)), and a 2024 *Scientific Reports* paper already paired 10-mer genomic features with **molecular-structure representations of 20 drugs over 32,312 genome–antibiotic pairs**, claiming a ~20% raw-accuracy gain ([Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-75973-2)). Read that paper in full before writing any novelty claim; it is the closest prior art for drug-aware Kp modelling.

What *does* work is transfer under imbalance. Ren et al. transferred a ciprofloxacin CNN's weights to three other drugs and saw external-set **MCC rise 0.06→0.41 (cefotaxime), 0.08→0.29 (ceftazidime), 0.11→0.26 (gentamicin)** and **AUROC 0.74→0.87 and 0.79→0.86 significantly**, while the source drug's own AUROC *fell* 0.93→0.89 ([PMC9686617](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9686617/)). The gains appeared in MCC and AUPRC on imbalanced external data, not in internal average AUROC. That is the shape of the win you should be targeting and the metric you should headline.

**The design that follows from all of this** is a two-model paper, not one architecture. The primary interpretable model is a **generalised additive model with pairwise interactions (EBM/GA2M) over the mechanism feature space**, where the per-feature shape functions *are* the model rather than a post-hoc approximation — which directly answers the published objection that **SHAP rankings inherit model bias, have no reference labels to check against, show only what the model relied on, and shift when top features are removed and the analysis is rerun** ([J Infect critique](https://www.journalofinfection.com/article/S0163-4453(25)00169-0/fulltext)). No published EBM/GA2M application to bacterial genomic AMR was found, and its pairwise terms are the natural representation for the carbapenemase×porin epistasis that a 1–5-rule Set Covering Machine cannot express — Kover is CPU-efficient and interpretable across **12 species and 56 antibiotics** ([arXiv 1612.01030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.01030)) but **missed `bla_CTX-M` entirely for amoxicillin** and trailed ResFinder 4.1 on external *E. coli* isolates ([Clin Microbiol Infect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1198743X2200283X)), so rule lists will lose your rare determinants across 17 drugs. The secondary model is a **mechanism-layer multi-task network with a factorised low-rank gene×drug interaction** — a shared gene-effect basis with per-drug loadings, explicitly *not* a shared trunk with 17 independent heads — whose evaluation endpoint is per-drug gain plotted against per-drug label count, with a label-subsampling experiment that artificially shrinks a data-rich drug to the size of your smallest. Reproduce the shared-trunk configuration that produced p = 0.374 and p = 0.48 as your own baseline and then beat it; that framing disarms the reviewer who knows those papers.

Finally, if you keep a knowledge-primed network, the reliability literature prescribes the controls. Retraining with different seeds gives divergent node importances, with replicate correlations in one system ranging from **R = 0.98 to −0.98**, and importance tracks graph centrality rather than biology ([npj Syst Biol Appl 9:50](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-023-00310-8)). The remedy is to **average over 20–30 seeds and subtract a shuffled-label control to produce differential attributions**, plus a randomly rewired mechanism mask and a parameter-matched dense comparator — a reusability report showed P-NET did beat randomly sparsified networks, so the biology does add something, but only that controlled comparison establishes it ([arXiv 2309.16645](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.16645)).

## An 80/20 split would buy about 0.005 AUROC — spend the budget on nested CV

The direct answer is **no**. Merging train and validation to reach 80% training data raises the training set by 25% relative to 64%. Empirical learning-curve exponents of **−0.07 to −0.35** ([Hestness et al., arXiv 1712.00409](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00409)) put the expected gain at **ΔAUROC ≈ 0.002–0.01** — smaller than the fold-to-fold standard deviation you will report, and far smaller than the random-to-lineage-blocked drop the same table will show. The empirical literature agrees: learning curves plateau well before the full training set is used, monotonic improvement appeared in only **6 of 19 conditions** in one *mSystems* study, accurate models have been built from **fewer than 500 diverse genomes**, and training-set size and performance correlate positively but without a quantified effect ([Front Cell Infect Microbiol 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2021.610348)). Decisively, **more data does not fix lineage confounding at all** ([PLOS Biol e3003539](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003539)), so the one failure mode you actually have is the one extra training data cannot touch.

Against that near-zero gain, merging costs you a clean inner loop for hyperparameter selection and feature selection, which is the thing reviewers check. The defensible version of "use all the data" is **nested cross-validation**, not a merged holdout: an outer loop for the estimate and an inner loop for tuning, with **feature selection redone inside each fold** and grouping by genomic distance; the published Kp precedent used **5-fold outer with 10-fold inner**, and the AMR benchmark used **10-fold nested CV with inner 9-fold selection** ([bbae206](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/25/3/bbae206/7665136)). If you refit a neural network on everything, reuse the median early-stopping epoch across folds or ship a fold/seed ensemble; stacking across fold models gave **+1.77% sensitivity and +3.20% specificity** with the fewest outright failures in the Kp context, and thresholds should be chosen from pooled out-of-fold predictions rather than a single validation split.

The deeper problem with your current protocol is not the ratio at all. **`fixed_test_multiseed` with seeds 1–9 and a frozen test set measures only train/val reshuffling variance.** It cannot estimate test-set sampling variance, so its error bars are systematically too narrow and cannot support a claim that you beat PanKA or Kover. Keep it, but relabel it an *optimisation-stability ablation* and say so explicitly. For the headline comparison, resample the test set — 10 repeats of 5-fold, or 10 repeats of the 64/16/20 draw with the test set redrawn — and analyse with the **corrected resampled t-test**, whose variance factor is **1/n + n_test/n_train** rather than the naive 1/n, because the plain paired t-test underestimates CV variance and inflates false positives ([Nadeau & Bengio](http://papers.neurips.cc/paper/1661-inference-for-the-generalization-error.pdf)). Also fix the leakage first: **collapse the 8 duplicated BioSamples to one genome each** before any split, since near-clonal isolates on both sides of a random split are precisely what inflates the numbers. No published source prescribes a numeric cgMLST or SNP deduplication threshold for AMR ML, so state your choice and show sensitivity to it.

## The protocol reviewers expect: three split tiers, VME/ME with intervals, calibration

Splits come in three tiers, all reported side by side. **Tier 1** is repeated random resampled splits for comparability with PanKA and the published benchmarks. **Tier 2** is lineage-blocked CV — MLST/sublineage-grouped `StratifiedGroupKFold` as the CPU-cheap version, a core-gene-tree clade holdout as the full version, built the way the critique paper built it, holding out one clade's phenotypes while other clades retain both ([PLOS Biol e3003539](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003539)). Budget for this: the 78-dataset benchmark **dropped 11 datasets because phylogeny-aware fold construction exceeded its runtime limits**. Note the honest counter-argument — blocking by phylogenetic distance can *induce extrapolation* by restricting the predictor combinations available in training, which can overestimate interpolation error ([Roberts et al., Ecography 2017](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecog.02881)) — which is exactly why all three tiers go in the same table. **Tier 3** is a fully external cohort by accession: KlebNET-GSP offers **12,167 matched genotype–phenotype KpSC isolates from 27 countries plus 7,030 externally contributed isolates**, and its rules-based ciprofloxacin classifier set the bar to beat at external **CA 93.12% (92.50–93.74), ME 8.65% (7.34–9.97), VME 6.20% (5.51–6.90)** — with the authors judging that accuracy **sufficient for surveillance but not for clinical use** ([bioRxiv 2025.09.24.678318](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.24.678318v1)). EuSCAPE (**>1,700 isolates, 244 hospitals, 32 countries, ENA PRJEB10018**; [Nat Microbiol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-019-0492-8)) gives multi-drug coverage, and the NCBI Pathogen Detection AST Browser gives breadth with the explicit caveat that **"NCBI does not vet the methods used or values supplied for AST data"** ([NCBI Insights](https://ncbiinsights.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2024/05/01/pathogen-detection-ast-browser/)). Budget conservatively for BV-BRC: of 18,645 quality-filtered Kp genomes, **only 4,976 had any AMR phenotype data** ([Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-24333-9)).

Baselines are non-negotiable and one of them is usually skipped. Beyond majority-class, ResFinder/Kleborate, PanKA and the naive-MTL ablation, the critique paper explicitly demands a **phylogenetic-placement or nearest-neighbour-by-ST baseline** to show the model adds information beyond lineage — any feature-importance story is credible only to the extent the model beats it. Metrics must be per drug, never only averaged: AUROC **and** AUPRC (AUPRC is what moves when the minority class is tiny), F1-macro as primary with susceptible-class precision and recall broken out, MCC, and **VME over resistant isolates and ME over susceptible isolates with 95% CIs**, benchmarked against FDA's criteria — the **upper 95% confidence limit on the very-major-discrepancy rate under 7.5% and the lower limit under 1.5%, major errors under 3%, categorical agreement ≥ 89.9%** ([FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/88069/download)). Cite ISO 20776-2:2021 correctly and separately: it **removed interpretive categories and categorical agreement** and added a bias requirement plus sensitivity/specificity provisions for qualitative devices ([ISO preview](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/ISO/preview_ISO+20776-2-2021.pdf)). Mark underpowered drugs explicitly — with **15 susceptible isolates in your cefuroxime test set, a single major error is 6.67%**, already double the FDA ceiling, and the interval spans roughly 0–20%; the benchmark's floor of **≥100 genomes per class** is a citable inclusion rule, and everything below it belongs in a separately labelled tier. Add calibration (reliability diagram, Brier score, ECE) — no AMR-ML paper found reports it, which means no in-field competition on that axis and a natural place to demonstrate a multi-task model's advantage on low-data drugs, where per-drug models are reliably overconfident.

Statistics: corrected resampled t-test for cross-validated comparisons, paired bootstrap (≥2,000 resamples, BCa intervals) for F1/MCC/VME/ME on fixed test sets, and **Holm–Bonferroni or Benjamini–Hochberg across the 17 drugs with both raw and adjusted p-values**. Reporting: supply a completed **DOME** checklist as the primary one for a genomics methods paper ([Nat Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01205-4)) and add **TRIPOD+AI**'s 27-item checklist plus its 13-item abstract checklist if you make any clinical-utility claim — which the VME/ME framing implicitly does ([PMC11019967](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11019967/)). Harmonise phenotypes to one stated breakpoint version and state how Intermediate was handled, since that single choice moves VME/ME by several points ([CMI 2024](https://www.clinicalmicrobiologyandinfection.org/article/S1198-743X(24)00339-2/fulltext)). Deposit the **exact fold assignments**, not just the code, as the benchmark did on Mendeley (doi 10.17632/6vc2msmsxj.2) — it is the cheapest possible defence against "you tuned on the test set". And read the EUCAST WGS-AST subcommittee's **2025/2026 update (doi 10.1016/j.cmi.2026.05.012), which newly adds sections on ML/AI** ([EUCAST subcommittee page](https://www.eucast.org/about-eucast/subcommittees/subcommittee-wgs/)) before writing the Discussion; its recommendations could not be retrieved in this research pass and it is the domain authority.

For the biological-insight claim, the field's accepted bar is tiered and assembled across papers rather than exemplified by one. A wet-lab-free ladder that reaches the realistic ceiling: the feature survives lineage-blocked CV; its association survives **conditioning on known determinants for that drug** — the design that let a *N. gonorrhoeae* study suppress lineage false positives and identify **RplD G70D, subsequently found in 5.42% of 4,850 isolates** ([Nat Commun 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19250-6)); it ranks stably across per-lineage refits and after top-feature removal; and it replicates in a held-out ST absent from training. That conditioning step is the most under-used and highest-value move available to you, because it is what separates "OmpK36 truncation predicts meropenem" from "OmpK36 truncation marks ST258, which carries `bla_KPC`". Anything clearing all tiers is a **candidate determinant requiring functional validation** and nothing stronger — a *M. tuberculosis* GWAS validated 13 non-canonical loci in an independent 792-isolate set but found that **`mymA`, which prior knockout work had linked to ethionamide resistance, failed to replicate** ([Nat Commun 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10110-6)).

## Conclusion

The framing that makes this publishable is an inversion: stop treating the random-split AUROC as the product and start treating **the gap between random and lineage-blocked performance as the measurement**. That gap is the quantity the field's central 2025 critique says is large and unmeasured, no one has reported it for *K. pneumoniae* across 17 antibiotics, and your 84–92% ST overlap guarantees you have a large one to report. A mechanism feature space is the natural partner for that framing rather than an independent contribution, because gene-integrity features — IS interruption, truncation, copy number, resolved alleles — are the features most likely to *survive* lineage blocking, since they encode the causal event rather than the clone that happens to carry it. The prediction worth stating in the paper and then testing: presence/absence features will lose most of their apparent signal under clade holdout while integrity features retain theirs, and the per-feature difference between the two regimes becomes a quantitative confounding score the field currently has no way to compute.

The realistic performance story is therefore narrow and honest. Expect parity on random splits, a clear win on lineage-blocked splits and on the low-data drugs where the factorised gene×drug model has something to borrow, external-cohort gains visible in MCC and AUPRC rather than AUROC, and the first mechanism-level attribution audit in bacterial AMR ML. Three things should happen before any code is written: confirm Bakta's actual GFF3 attribute schema against a real output file, since the IS Dbxref and pseudogene conventions the whole feature layer depends on are undocumented in the README and the canonical docs URL is dead; read the 2024 *Scientific Reports* Kp drug-structure CNN in full, because it determines what remains claimable about drug-aware modelling; and read the EUCAST 2026 WGS-AST update, which is both the domain authority and now explicitly covers ML. Treat the 2026-dated DOIs cited here as requiring verification before they enter a reference list.
