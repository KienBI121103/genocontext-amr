## Q1. Population-structure confounding in bacterial genotype→phenotype ML

### Takeaway
The single most directly relevant paper is Yu, Wheeler & Barquist (PLOS Biology 2025), which analysed >24,000 genomes from five pathogens including *K. pneumoniae* and showed that when resistance is confounded with phylogeny, LightGBM models trained on random splits collapse on held-out clades, that more training data does not rescue them, and that top features differ almost entirely between clades — the signature of lineage-marker learning. Your 84–92% same-ST overlap between train and test is exactly the regime that paper warns about.

### Cited findings

**The core confounding paper**
- Yu, Wheeler & Barquist analysed **over 24,000 whole genome sequences and AMR phenotypes from five diverse pathogens, using pathological training data where resistance is confounded with phylogeny**, and showed **the resulting ML models perform poorly and that increasing the training sample size fails to rescue performance** — [PLOS Biology 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003539); [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/); preprint [bioRxiv 2025.01.07.631773](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.07.631773v1)
- Species covered: *E. coli*, ***K. pneumoniae***, *S. enterica*, *S. aureus*, *S. pneumoniae*; **3,204 to 7,188 genomes per species**; **27 antibiotics**; species-drug combinations with **<1,000 genomes or either phenotype above 80% were excluded**; mean ~**2,700 genomes per combination, ~44% resistant** — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- Model class and features: **LightGBM, using SNPs and Roary presence/absence features**; **6,740 models** in total; **a comprehensive analysis of 6,740 models identifies species- and drug-specific effects on model accuracy** — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- Scheme A (train with resistant or susceptible isolates withheld from one clade, test on that clade): models trained on biased data had **"much lower" AUC than random splits**. Scheme B (add the remaining clades to training, still excluding one phenotype from the test clade): **AUC improved over Scheme A, but precision and recall stayed strongly biased** — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- Evidence for lineage-marker learning: **ML models continued to conflate lineage markers with genuine indicators of resistance, even in the presence of a large training dataset**; the supporting evidence is **skewed precision and recall when one phenotype is removed from the test clade, plus minimal overlap in top features across clades** — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- Concrete feature-instability example: for ciprofloxacin, **parC ranked highly in most *E. coli* and *S. aureus* clades, while gyrA appeared in only one *S. aureus* clade** — i.e. a canonical, mechanistically certain determinant was *not* recovered consistently across clades — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- **Gram-negative species showed lower AUC**, which the authors attribute to the outer membrane — relevant because your entire dataset is Gram-negative with porin-mediated mechanisms — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- Feature-representation caveat from the reviews: **hybrid SNP and presence/absence features may miss within-gene variation in accessory genes**; the authors ran a **unitig analysis only on a subset of *K. pneumoniae* (1,429 genomes)** and say it supports their results — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- **Dissenting view inside the same record:** Reviewer 2 argued the lineage-marker inference is not fully established, since **lower performance could reflect less informative data in some clades, not only lineage confounding** — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)

**Deconfounding methods recommended by that paper**
Phylogeny-aware cross-validation testing on held-out clades; balanced test sets and rigorous metrics; **lineage-aware ML that borrows mixed-effects models from bacterial GWAS (e.g. pyseer)**; **phylogenetic placement as a baseline, to show whether ML adds anything beyond phylogenetic structure**; feature selection informed by phylogenetic conservation; and more diverse, globally representative sampling including low- and middle-income countries — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)

**Supporting / review literature**
- **Nonrandom sampling shrinks the effective size of a genomic dataset regardless of how many genomes it contains**; **samples from similar locations, times, and habitat types can produce large sets of very similar genomes, and the resulting population structure can be a significant confounder in the ML model** — [Clin Microbiol Rev, 10.1128/cmr.00179-21](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/cmr.00179-21)
- Open-problems survey framing the same issue for bacterial genomics ML — [arXiv:2502.07749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07749)
- A core-genome-only model that **dropped known resistance genes** reportedly retained **nearly identical accuracies and error rates across all antibiotics compared with the model built from the full genomes** (~92%) — [PLOS Comput Biol, 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008319](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1008319). I did not fetch the full text; treat the exact accuracy figure as needing verification.

**K. pneumoniae-specific lineage-aware splitting**
- **ST-blocked CV, closest analogue to your setup:** an ertapenem project groups **isolates strictly by Sequence Type (ST) using 5-fold StratifiedGroupKFold** over **3,702 isolates**, reporting **ROC-AUC ≈ 0.81 (attention-based MIL)** and **≈ 0.80 (hybrid MIL + XGBoost)** — [github.com/AMorales22T/AMT-Klebsiella-ertapenem](https://github.com/AMorales22T/AMT-Klebsiella-ertapenem). **Caveat: self-reported figures in a code repository, not peer-reviewed.** The isolate count and design are strikingly close to your 3,949-genome K. pneumoniae problem; worth reading its grouping code directly.
- **Clade-blocked CV:** a 2026 meropenem pipeline partitions isolates by clade using **Hamming distance in gene presence–absence profiles** so that **closely related genomes do not appear simultaneously in training and test sets**; **554 unique genomes after deduplication**, with external validation against **seven separate BioProjects holding 305 isolates** — [Bioinformatics Advances vbag193](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformaticsadvances/article/6/1/vbag193/8746877); [PMC13532757](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13532757/); [PubMed 42683100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42683100/)
- Additional K. pneumoniae ML preprint — [bioRxiv 2024.12.10.627815](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.10.627815v1)

**pyseer / lineage-effect machinery (concrete options)**
- pyseer tool paper — [Bioinformatics 34(24):4310, bty539](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article-pdf/34/24/4310/27088675/bty539.pdf); usage docs — [pyseer.readthedocs.io](https://pyseer.readthedocs.io/en/master/usage.html) (docs reviewed were v1.3.10, Nov 2023)
- **Estimating lineage effects provides an estimate of the risk of false positive GWAS findings related to the confounding from the population structure**; a strong signal **might signify that there are high-risk clones for resistance or virulence phenotype**; **variants associated with both the phenotype and a lineage can be prioritized for further analysis**; the method **is based on the procedure used in bugwas** — [pyseer docs](https://pyseer.readthedocs.io/en/master/usage.html)
- Original lineage-effect method: **Identifying lineage effects when controlling for population structure improves power in bacterial association studies** — [arXiv:1510.06863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.06863) (Earle et al., bugwas)
- **Whole-genome elastic net handles structure through `--sequence-reweighting` and `--lineage-clusters`**; sequence reweighting means **each sample's contribution to the fitting is downweighted by the prevalence of the strain** — directly applicable to your ST258 (612) / ST307 (532) / ST15 (305) imbalance — [pyseer docs](https://pyseer.readthedocs.io/en/master/usage.html)
- **The elastic net performed better in situations where the heritability was low or causal variants were spread out across the genome**; its **false-positive rate was typically <5% and was robust across the ranges of heritability tested, though it increased slightly with larger sample sizes**; and **a dual approach of variable selection with the elastic net followed by ranking results with the linear mixed model may be useful**, which **is possible in a single step in pyseer** — [mBio / PMC7343994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7343994/); preprint [bioRxiv 852426](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/852426v1.full.pdf)
- **LMM route:** **FaST-LMM ... allows to correct for population structure by adding a kinship matrix as a random effect**; **fixed-effect route:** **the first 4 components of the multidimensional scaling of the pairwise Mash distance matrix**; in one *S. aureus* study **the association between the first 10 components and the bacterial GWAS outcomes was computed by running Pyseer with the '--lineage' option** — [Cell Reports Medicine / ScienceDirect S221112472301080X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221112472301080X)
- Heritability/prediction framework in bacteria — [NAR Genomics & Bioinformatics 4(1):lqac011](https://academic.oup.com/nargab/article/4/1/lqac011/6534364)
- Worked pipeline for population-structure and lineage effects — [github.com/nrclaudio/EcoRef](https://github.com/nrclaudio/EcoRef)

### Inferences
- With 84–92% of test isolates sharing an ST with training isolates, your random-split AUCs are best read as *upper bounds contaminated by lineage leakage*. The PLOS Biology design gives you a ready-made experimental protocol: report random-split, ST-blocked, and held-out-clade metrics side by side, and treat the **gap** between them as your quantitative estimate of lineage confounding — this is the paper's own logic applied to your data.
- The two independent K. pneumoniae groups that did block by lineage (ST-grouped StratifiedGroupKFold; clade-blocked by Hamming distance) converge on **ROC-AUC ≈ 0.80–0.81 for carbapenem phenotypes**. That is a useful sanity anchor: a lineage-blocked carbapenem model substantially above ~0.85 in your hands should prompt a leakage audit rather than celebration.
- The PLOS Biology "phylogenetic placement as a baseline" recommendation is the cheapest high-value control you can add: a nearest-neighbour-by-ST or nearest-neighbour-by-core-SNP classifier. Any feature-importance story is only credible to the extent the model beats that baseline.
- The ciprofloxacin `parC`/`gyrA` instability result argues against trusting a single global importance ranking. Per-clade or per-ST importance stability (does the feature rank highly in *every* held-out-lineage refit?) is a much stronger criterion, and is implementable on CPU.

### Gaps
- The PLOS Biology full text did not expose the headline **numeric** AUC values for random vs. phylogeny-aware splits — **those appear only in Fig 1D and S2/S3 Data**, which I could not read. You will need the supplementary data for a quantitative "inflation" figure.
- A figure surfaced in search results — *"control accuracy was on average 0.28 higher for all drugs excluding AMP, AMX and CET"* for an *E. coli* held-out-clade comparison — could not be attributed to a specific URL with confidence. Do not cite it without tracing it.
- I found **no** paper applying formal causal/invariant learning (IRM, invariant causal prediction, environment-based risk minimisation) to bacterial AMR genomics, and **no** paper applying conditional permutation importance to AMR genomic features. Both appear to be genuine open ground rather than established practice.
- No study quantified how much ST-blocked CV changes performance *on K. pneumoniae specifically across many antibiotics* — the two K. pneumoniae examples each cover a single carbapenem.

## Q2. Interpretable model classes for AMR

### Takeaway
Rule-based Set Covering Machines (Kover) are the only interpretable class with a large, published AMR track record (12 species, 56 antibiotics) and explicit sample-compression generalisation guarantees, but they miss epistasis and under-detect rare determinants. GBM+SHAP dominates recent practice and has drawn a formal published critique for exactly the reasons that matter to you. I found **no** published application of Explainable Boosting Machines / GA2M or neural additive models to bacterial genomic AMR.

### Cited findings

**Set Covering Machine / Kover**
- Models are **compact conjunctions (logical-AND) or disjunctions (logical-OR) of one to five Boolean rules derived from k-mer features of WGS data**; SCM is **an inherently interpretable ML algorithm, used to generate transparent, human-readable predictions** — [Brief Bioinform / PMC13222522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13222522/) (doi [10.1093/bib/bbag269](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag269))
- Original work proposed **strong performance guarantees, based on sample compression theory, for rule-based learning algorithms that produce highly interpretable models**; **highly accurate models were obtained for 12 species and 56 antibiotics, and their interpretation revealed known resistance mechanisms, as well as some potential new ones**; the implementation is **disk-based ... both memory and computationally efficient ... turnkey, requires no prior knowledge of machine learning** — [arXiv:1612.01030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.01030); code at [github.com/aldro61/kover](https://github.com/aldro61/kover). The CPU-efficiency point matters for your CPU-only server.
- In *Salmonella*, SCM models performed **similarly to logistic regression and considerably better than direct tallying under FDA-style metrics** — [mSystems 10.1128/msystems.00211-19](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/msystems.00211-19)
- **Limitations (review table):** **limited model expressiveness, misses epistatic interactions** — [PMC13222522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13222522/). This is decisive for your use case: KPC + OmpK36-GD, or ESBL + porin loss, are *multiplicative* mechanisms a 1–5-rule conjunction handles poorly.
- **Independent external validation:** against *E. coli* clinical isolates, **KOVER-AMR** trailed **ResFinder 4.1** and covered **fewer of the 14 antimicrobials tested (11 versus 13)** — [Clin Microbiol Infect, S1198743X2200283X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1198743X2200283X)
- **Rare-determinant blind spot:** Kover can only learn markers that occur often enough in its training data, so **the amoxicillin models missed bla_CTX-M genes** — [S1198743X2200283X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1198743X2200283X)
- **Interpretability ≠ causation:** Kover does not require a known biological mechanism to select predictors, so a selected rule does not by itself establish a causal link — [S1198743X2200283X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1198743X2200283X)
- **Cross-lineage/species fragility:** a 2026 benchmark reports Kover accuracy **drops sharply when evaluated on held-out species**, and that **ampicillin results were unstable, with validation F1 ranging from 0.03 to 0.87 across runs** — [arXiv:2603.11141](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11141)

**GBM + SHAP, and its documented pitfalls**
- Formal published critique: **Limitations of XGBoost-SHAP integration for interpretable machine learning in antimicrobial resistance prediction** — [J Infect, S0163-4453(25)00169-0](https://www.journalofinfection.com/article/S0163-4453(25)00169-0/fulltext). Its specific objections:
  - importance rankings have **no reference labels to check them against, unlike predictions**
  - a ranked SNP **shows what the model relied on, not proof that the locus drives resistance**
  - because SHAP is computed from the fitted model, **any model bias can carry through into the explanations**
  - **rankings reportedly shift when the top-ranked features are removed and the analysis is rerun** — a directly runnable stability test for your pipeline
  - **local validation and oversight are needed before clinical deployment**
- Applied examples that did validate explanations, and how:
  - *E. coli*, 809 strains, 1D CNN + XGBoost ensemble; **SHAP run on the XGBoost component for four antibiotics**, then **top SNPs placed on the K-12 MG1655 reference genome as a biological check** — [arXiv:2509.23552](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23552); also [ACM 10.1145/3773656.3773682](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3773656.3773682)
  - *P. aeruginosa*, k-mer pipeline; best model a **decision tree with 79% accuracy and 84% sensitivity**, with XAI used to find **antibiotic-specific k-mer signatures** — [Bioinformatics Advances vbaf190](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformaticsadvances/article/5/1/vbaf190/8239948); [PMC12380447](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12380447/)
  - *M. tuberculosis*: **SHAP-derived importance agreed with motifs found through attention visualization** — a cross-method agreement check — reported via [PMC13222522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13222522/)
  - *H. pylori*: XGBoost best, interpreted with SHAP, genome-wide genetic variation — [PMC11934168](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11934168/)
  - ESKAPEE preprint: **18,916 genome assemblies and 40 antibiotics**, with **SHAP computed separately for each antibiotic–genus pair** — [Research Square rs-7190203](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-7190203/v1) (preprint)
- Elastic net as an interpretable AMR/GWAS workhorse with structure correction built in — see Q1: [PMC7343994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7343994/), [pyseer docs](https://pyseer.readthedocs.io/en/master/usage.html)

### Inferences
- The validation methods actually used in the literature form a short, reproducible checklist you can adopt wholesale: (1) map top features to a reference genome/known-determinant catalogue; (2) check agreement between two independent explanation methods; (3) rerun after deleting top features and test rank stability; (4) evaluate on an external collection. None requires GPU.
- EBM/GA2M is a defensible choice *despite* the absence of AMR precedent, and the absence is itself a contribution. Its specific advantage over GBM+SHAP maps onto the J Infect critique: in a GA2M the per-feature shape functions **are** the model, not a post-hoc approximation, so the "SHAP inherits model bias" objection does not apply in the same form. Its pairwise interaction terms (the "2M" in GA2M) are also the natural representation for the carbapenemase × porin epistasis that SCM cannot express. I have no source establishing that EBM outperforms alternatives on AMR data; this is a design argument, not a cited result.
- Given the Kover rare-determinant failure (missing `bla_CTX-M` for amoxicillin), a rule-list or SCM model on your data would likely miss low-prevalence determinants across your 17 antibiotics. A sparse/elastic-net or EBM layer over curated annotation-derived features is likelier to retain them.
- SHAP on correlated features is the acute risk in your setting specifically because annotation-derived features are heavily collinear within mobile elements (an IS26-flanked array yields many co-occurring features), and because lineage membership correlates with whole gene complements. Conditional/grouped importance would be the principled remedy, but see Gaps.

### Gaps
- **No published EBM/GA2M or neural additive model application to bacterial genomic AMR was found** in two targeted searches. If this matters to your framing, treat it as novel and validate accordingly rather than citing precedent.
- **No AMR-specific study of conditional permutation importance or grouped-SHAP for correlated genomic features** was found. The general ML critique exists ([J Infect](https://www.journalofinfection.com/article/S0163-4453(25)00169-0/fulltext)) but the correlated-feature remedy is not demonstrated on AMR data.
- I did not find a head-to-head benchmark of SCM vs. elastic net vs. GBM **under lineage-blocked CV** on the same K. pneumoniae dataset. That comparison appears to be unperformed and would be a genuine contribution.
- No source quantified how rule-list / falling-rule-list methods perform on AMR genomics.

## Q3. K. pneumoniae mechanisms detectable from Bakta annotation

### Takeaway
A large fraction of clinically important K. pneumoniae resistance is **not** gene presence/absence: it is gene *loss*, *truncation*, *IS interruption*, *copy number*, and *allele identity*. Porin status, regulator disruption, and β-lactamase gene dosage together account for most of the carbapenem/cephalosporin/pip-tazo phenotypes that a presence/absence model gets wrong — and all three leave signatures in GFF3 coordinates, pseudogene flags, and IS Dbxrefs rather than in gene names.

### What Bakta actually emits (format basis)
From Bakta's own repository documentation — [github.com/oschwengers/bakta](https://github.com/oschwengers/bakta):
- CDS are cross-referenced to **RefSeq (WP_\*), UniRef100 (UniRef100_\*), and UniParc (UPI\*) identifiers via exact sequence matching**; the database is built from **UniRef100, UniRef90, and UniRef50 clusters**, with **assignment to UniRef90 or UniRef50 when alignment identity passes 0.9 or 0.5** respectively; the DB is **enriched with GO, COG, and EC dbxrefs**
- **Pseudogenes are a listed feature type**, with a **`--skip-pseudo` option**; detection works by **searching for reference protein clusters using hypothetical CDS as seeds, running translated alignments (blastx) against up- and downstream-extended regions, and analyzing the alignments for causes and effects of pseudogenization**
- **AMR genes are annotated with NCBI's AMRFinderPlus as part of an expert-system step, which can override generic annotations** — this is the origin of your `blaKPC-2` / `AAC(6')-Ib-cr` names
- **ISfinder is one of the sources used to pre-annotate IPS and PSC protein sequences** — the origin of IS-derived annotations
- **`<prefix>.faa` contains CDS/sORF amino acid sequences as FASTA**; hypothetical CDS are written to a separate **`<prefix>.hypotheticals.faa`**

**Caveat — verify locally before building on it:** the Bakta README **does not document the GFF3 attribute schema**, does not confirm SO terms, and does not state how pseudogenes or IS elements are written into GFF3 attributes. The canonical docs URL `bakta.readthedocs.io/en/latest/BAKTA.html` returned **HTTP 404**. Your stated `IS:<name>` Dbxref convention and pseudogene flags should be confirmed against a real Bakta 1.9.4 output file (the binary is present at `/home/kiennm/AMR/AMR_GNN/.pixi/envs/default/bin/bakta`) before the feature extractor depends on them.

### Mechanism-by-mechanism evidence

**OmpK35 / OmpK36 porin loss and truncation — carbapenems, cephalosporins, pip-tazo, amp-sulbactam**
- Single-gene knockouts in a clinical isolate: **the single deletion of ompK36 resulted in MIC shifts of cefazolin, cephalothin, and cefoxitin from susceptible to resistant, while the single deletion of ompK35 had no significant effect**; the **double deletion ... led to 8- and 16-fold increases in the MICs of meropenem and cefepime, respectively**; overall **the combination of OmpK36 loss resulted in a 2- to 8-fold or more increase in the MICs of penicillin/β-lactamase inhibitor, cephalosporins, and carbapenems** — [AAC 10.1128/aac.01275-10](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.01275-10); [PMC3067157](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3067157/)
- **Conflicting earlier report:** **the loss of the expression of one porin, either OmpK35 or OmpK36, did not significantly alter the MICs of the tested isolates** — [S0924857918300980](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924857918300980). Strain background evidently modulates the effect; do not model porin loss as a fixed-effect constant.
- **Porin loss alone is usually insufficient:** **the increased production of ESBL or AmpC is a prerequisite for meropenem or imipenem resistance, as porin loss alone results only in marginally elevated meropenem and/or imipenem MICs still within the susceptible range**; **all non-carbapenemase-producing Klebsiella pneumoniae (NCPK) isolates lacked at least one of the two major porins**; **porin loss in NCPK was due to varied mechanisms, including point mutations, frameshift mutations or insertion sequences**; **strains lacking both porins exhibit higher ertapenem resistance than strains expressing one porin** — [S0924857918300980](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924857918300980); [PubMed 29621592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29621592/). **This is an explicit epistasis requirement — a carbapenemase/ESBL × porin interaction term.**
- **Truncation, with complementation proof:** in a *K. quasipneumoniae* isolate, **N7039 harbored a wild-type ompK35, but ompK36 was truncated (Y325stop)**; **complementation with wild-type ompK36 significantly reduced carbapenem MICs, by more than 256-fold for ertapenem and 64- to 128-fold for the others** — [PMC12691662](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12691662/)
- **IS insertional inactivation:** two isolates had **identical insertions in ompK35 and ompK36, resulting in disruption of these key porin genes**; notably one remained carbapenem-susceptible because **the up-regulation of phoE in KP-1 apparently compensated for porin loss** — [AAC 10.1128/aac.00285-06](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.00285-06)
- **L3 loop GD insertion (sequence-level, not presence/absence):** **By solving the crystal structure of a clinical ST258 OmpK36 variant we provide direct structural evidence of pore constriction, mediated by a di-amino acid (Gly115-Asp116) insertion into loop 3, restricting diffusion of both nutrients (e.g. lactose) and Carbapenems**; **In the presence of KPC-2 this results in a 16-fold increase in MIC to Meropenem**; it **confers a significant in vivo fitness cost in a murine model of ventilator-associated pneumonia**; and **the GD insertion and other sequence variations in L3 of OmpK36_ST258_ are correlated with increased resistance in clinical isolates from international KP collections** — [Nat Commun 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-11756-y); [PMC6718652](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6718652/)
- A related **Asp137-Thr138 duplication in the L3 loop of OmpK36**, alongside a truncated OmpK35, in ST258 — [AAC 10.1128/aac.01816-19](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.01816-19)
- Additional OmpK36 variant and OXA-48 context: [AAC 10.1128/aac.01301-09](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/aac.01301-09); [PMC8532661](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8532661/)
- **Porin loss + β-lactamase gene dosage together:** **increased copy numbers of blaOXA-48-containing plasmids, combined with OmpK36 loss, contributed to high carbapenem MICs without the involvement of OmpK35 or other porins or efflux systems** — [PMC12217476](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12217476/)

**blaKPC copy number, Tn4401 isoforms, IS26 amplification — carbapenems and CZA**
- **Tn4401 relocation:** **the increased expression of blaKPC-3 and blaSHV-12 in the resistant isolate was traced to Tn4401 moving into a second plasmid, pIncX3** in a porin-deficient strain with increased efflux — [AAC 10.1128/aac.00989-17](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.00989-17); [PMC5610495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610495/)

**SHV alleles, SHV copy number, promoter variants — pip-tazo, amp-sulbactam, ceftazidime**
- Recent, highly relevant study of 14 unrelated TZP-resistant isolates: **in the remaining 10, resistance correlated with plasmid-borne bla_SHV-1 copies**; of the other four, **two had a bla_OXA-1 gene, one also carrying an ompK35 porin gene mutation; two others had the same mutation in the promoter of the chromosomal copy of bla_SHV usually associated with overexpression** — [AAC 10.1128/aac.01682-25](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/aac.01682-25); [PubMed 41874364](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41874364/)

- **Allele identity alone is a weak predictor:** an analysis of SHV diversity did **not support a simple association between Ambler site 69 mutations and BLI resistance in K. pneumoniae**, e.g. **SHV-52 isolates (35Q, 69I) were TZP-resistant in 0 of 12 cases tested**, even though **mutations 69I and 69V have been thought to explain BLI resistance of variants SHV-49, SHV-52, SHV-92 and SHV-203** — [SHV diversity analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385108865_Diversity_functional_classification_and_genotyping_of_SHV_b-lactamases_in_Klebsiella_pneumoniae) (ResearchGate record; prefer the primary journal version if you can locate it)
- Classic SHV × porin interplay: **High-level expression of chromosomally encoded SHV-1 β-lactamase and an outer membrane protein change confer resistance to ceftazidime and piperacillin-tazobactam** — [AAC 44(2):362-367](https://journals.asm.org/doi/abs/10.1128/aac.44.2.362-367.2000)

**ramR / acrR / marR disruption and efflux — tetracycline (and tigecycline)**
- Regulatory logic: **RamA encoded by ramA acts as a global transcriptional activator for AcrAB-TolC, while ramR encodes the local repressor RamR for ramA**; **the transcription level of AcrAB/OqxAB is also regulated by its local repressor AcrR and OqxR, which are encoded by acrR and oqxR** — [Spectrum 10.1128/spectrum.03204-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03204-24); [PMC12131797](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12131797/)
- **IS insertion into ramR, with complementation proof:** a serial ST11 isolate carried **insertion sequence (IS) element ISKpn18 in ramR**, with **tigecycline MIC 16 mg/L compared with 4 mg/L in the earlier isolates**, and **transforming a parent strain with a plasmid carrying ramR enhanced tigecycline susceptibility** — [Spectrum 10.1128/spectrum.03204-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03204-24)
- In ST512 KPC-3: **the RamR sequence of KP2-R, KP6-R, and KP7-R was disrupted by insertion of the ISKpn18 element, whereas KP5-R had a premature termination**, alongside ribosomal S10 protein mutations — [AAC 10.1128/aac.01803-13](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.01803-13)
- **ramR was interrupted by Insertion sequence (IS) Kpn14 in XDX51, with this strain exhibiting upregulation of ramA, acrA and acrB expression relative to comparator isolates** — [Sci Rep 10.1038/s41598-024-55705-2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-55705-2)
- **acrR IS insertion:** **Mutations in the acrR gene were observed in 14 isolates, and 12 isolates have IS5 insertion element into the nucleotide position 276–277 of the gene** — [PLOS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0119064](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0119064)
- **Additive effect:** **Of 26 TNSKP isolates, 8 harbored ramR and acrR mutations simultaneously**, suggesting **the potential role of acrR in augmenting the level of resistance conferred by ramR mutation** — [PubMed 28368073](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28368073/)
- **Important negative control for interpretation:** **most of these mutations, especially I141T and A19V in RamR and R13W in AcrR, have been reported to have no effects on overexpression of RamA** — [Spectrum 10.1128/spectrum.03204-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03204-24). **Disruption (IS insertion / frameshift / premature stop) is the predictive signal; missense is not.** That distinction maps perfectly onto a pseudogene-flag + IS-Dbxref feature and poorly onto a SNP feature.
- Co-occurring *tet(A)* variants: **three isolates with acrR mutations ... harbored both a ramR mutation and tet(A)** — [AAC 10.1128/aac.00391-17](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.00391-17); further ramR evidence — [AAC 10.1128/aac.00085-10](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.00085-10), [AAC 10.1128/aac.03808-14](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.03808-14), [S2213716520300138](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213716520300138); antibiotic-exposure-induced regulator changes — [PLOS ONE 10.1371/journal.pone.0315847](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315847)

**gyrA / parC QRDR and PMQR — fluoroquinolones**
- Bushehr, Iran (40 ciprofloxacin-resistant isolates): **35 (87.5%) and 34 (85%) of the ciprofloxacin-resistant isolates had mutations in these genes** (gyrA, parC); **90% of isolates have at least one mutation in QRDR**; and **13 (32.5%), 7 (17.5%), 40 (100%), and 25 (62.5%) isolates harbored qnrB, qnrS, oqxA and aac(6')-Ib-cr genes** — [BMC Microbiol 10.1186/s12866-024-03383-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12866-024-03383-5); [PMC11256406](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11256406/)
- Chinese bloodstream CRKP (149 isolates): **QRDR mutations were found in 75.2%**; **No mutations in the QRDRs were found in 31 ciprofloxacin susceptible CRKP isolates**; PMQR distribution **qnrS1 (43.0%), aac(6′)-Ib-cr (16.1%), qnrB4 (6.0%)**; **the co-existence rate of PMQR determinants and mutations in the QRDRs of gyrA and parC reached 68.5% (61/89)**; and **the co-existence of PMQR genes and mutations in the QRDRs can increase the resistance level of CRKP to fluoroquinolones** — [BMC Microbiol 10.1186/s12866-021-02238-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12866-021-02238-7)
- Heilongjiang CRKP ST11/ST76: **Ib-cr was the most frequently detected PMQR gene, detected in 95% of strains tested (38 of 40)** — [S1567134820301507](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1567134820301507)
- Malaysia ESBL-producing: **aac(6')-Ib-cr was the most common PMQR gene detected in this study (n = 61)**; resistance linked most strongly to **the presence of multiple mutations in gyrA and parC regions** — [PMC4000930](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4000930/)
- Hotspot positions: **the usual hotspots are position 83 or 87 in gyrA and position 80 within parC**; in one series **the gyrA Ser83Leu change appeared in all 60 resistant isolates sequenced**, with **Asp87Asn** second most common — [S2213716518300845](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2213716518300845)
- Mechanism of the PMQR genes: **Qnr proteins belong to the pentapeptide repeat family and protect DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV from quinolone inhibition, and the AAC(6')-Ib-cr enzyme can modify ciprofloxacin with a piperazinyl substituent, reducing its activity** — [BMC Microbiol 10.1186/s12866-021-02238-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12866-021-02238-7)
- **Critical note for your pipeline:** `aac(6')-Ib-cr` vs. plain `aac(6')-Ib` differ by two residues and have *different* drug spectra. AMRFinderPlus-derived Bakta names distinguish them; k-mer or presence/absence features built on gene family would not.

**nfsA / nfsB / ribE and oqxAB — nitrofurantoin**
- **Separate inactivating mutations in both nfsA and nfsB are typically required for clinical levels of resistance and, indeed, are observed in clinically resistant organisms** — [Nat Commun Biol / npj Antimicrob Resist 10.1038/s44259-024-00056-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44259-024-00056-1). **This is an explicit AND-interaction, naturally expressed as an EBM pairwise term or an SCM conjunction.**
- K. pneumoniae-specific: three clones carried **nfsA R203C, nfsB R207L, and ribE R58H/D203E** plus **oqxAB**, with resistance at **128–512 mg/L**; **Mutations in nfsA, nfsB & ribE collectively confer high-level resistance to nitrofurantoin**; two clones lacking nfsA/nfsB changes had **intermediate resistance to NFT (64 mg/l), suggesting that the D203E mutation in ribE could cause low-level NFT resistance**, with the authors calling for **further complementation and mutagenesis studies** — [Future Sci OA 10.4155/fsoa-2017-0156](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.4155/fsoa-2017-0156)
- Efflux contribution: **AcrAB and OqxAB contribute to nitrofurantoin resistance in an uropathogenic Klebsiella pneumoniae isolate**, where **addition of the efflux pump inhibitors PAβN and CCCP increased the susceptibility of NRKP to NIT, leading to a four-fold decrease** — [S0924857919301451](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924857919301451)
- **oqxAB genes are a complementary NFT-R mechanism that is adding up to nfsA, nfsB and ribE mutations to yield high-level NFT resistance** — [Future Sci OA 10.4155/fsoa-2017-0156](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.4155/fsoa-2017-0156)
- High-level resistant K. pneumoniae isolate: altered expression of nitroreductase and transport genes **including oqxR, the transcriptional regulator of the oqxAB efflux pump** — [PMC10805014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10805014/)
- E. coli reference for nfsA/nfsB/ribE alterations — [PMC8767348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8767348/); review — [Karger, Med Princ Pract 34(2):121](https://karger.com/mpp/article/34/2/121/915372/Unlocking-Nitrofurantoin-Understanding-Molecular)
- **Caveat:** the K. pneumoniae ribE findings rest on a small number of clones and are not functionally confirmed — [Future Sci OA 10.4155/fsoa-2017-0156](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.4155/fsoa-2017-0156)
- **Important for K. pneumoniae specifically:** `oqxAB` is **chromosomally intrinsic** in this species — note it was found in **100%** of isolates in the Iranian series above ([PMC11256406](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11256406/)). Presence/absence of `oqxA`/`oqxB` therefore carries ~zero information in K. pneumoniae; only **copy number or regulator (`oqxR`) status** does.

**mgrB disruption — colistin (noted as likely out of your 17-antibiotic scope)**
- IS-in-*mgrB* frequencies vary widely by region: Taiwan **17/49 (34.7%)** and **10/31 (32.2%)**; Iran **30% (n=6/20)**; China **9 of 11**; Greece **93.75% (n=15/16) ... harboured ISKpn26-like element disruption in mgrB** — [PMC8796155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8796155/)
- Saudi Arabia: **ISKpn14 was the major IS and 59% of isolates have ISKpn14-mediated mgrB gene disruption** — [Infect Drug Resist 10.2147/IDR.S161146](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2147/IDR.S161146)
- Brazil (counting insertions, not isolates): **IS5-family sequences predominantly (76%; 32/42) disrupted mgrB, with IS903 and IS903B accounting for 33% (14/42) and 26% (11/42)** — [S2213716520302897](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213716520302897)
- **Causal evidence:** **IS elements including ISKpn26, ISKpn14, and IS903B cloned into a plasmid vector and transformed into a colistin susceptible K. pneumoniae isolate increased the frequency of colistin resistance** — [PMC8796155](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8796155/); and in a clinical case **colistin resistance emerg[ed] due to disruption of mgrB by IS903 after 8 days of colistin-based combination therapy** — [PMC5913667](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5913667/); Vietnam series — [S0924857917304296](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924857917304296); missense example MgrB W20S — [PMC8849082](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8849082/)
- **Why this matters even if colistin is out of scope:** *mgrB* is the best-documented "short gene disrupted by IS" case in K. pneumoniae and is therefore the ideal **positive control** for validating your IS-interruption detector, because the ground truth is unusually well established.

### Inferences
- **Gene presence/absence misses, by mechanism class:** (a) *loss-of-function* mechanisms where the informative state is absence/truncation of a gene present in nearly all genomes (ompK35/36, ramR, acrR, nfsA, nfsB, mgrB) — presence/absence is near-constant so the feature is uninformative; (b) *dosage* mechanisms (blaKPC, blaSHV, blaOXA-48) where the gene is present in both resistant and susceptible isolates and only copy number differs; (c) *allele-identity* mechanisms (SHV variants, KPC-2 vs KPC-14/-51/-189, aac(6')-Ib vs -Ib-cr, gyrA/parC QRDR) where the gene name is shared; (d) *context* mechanisms (Tn4401 isoform, IS26 pseudocompound transposon structure, plasmid vs chromosome) where the same gene has different expression depending on flanking sequence.
- **Copy number is approximable from GFF3 alone** by counting CDS features whose `gene`/`product` match the same AMR determinant across all contigs — the SHV paper's finding that **bla_SHV copy number correlated with TZP, AMC, and CAZ MICs** ([AAC 10.1128/aac.01682-25](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/aac.01682-25)) means a simple duplicate-count feature is likely to be one of the highest-value additions for your pip-tazo and amp-sulbactam targets. Tandem duplicates that assemble into a single collapsed contig will be undercounted, so this is a lower bound.
- **Truncation is computable from GFF3 + FAA** without any read data: compare the CDS length (GFF3 `end - start`) or the FAA protein length against the species-typical length for that `gene`/UniRef90 cluster. OmpK36 is ~360 aa; a Y325stop variant like the one with a >256-fold ertapenem effect ([PMC12691662](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12691662/)) is detectable as a length outlier.
- **IS-interruption is computable from coordinates alone:** an IS/transposase CDS (identifiable by `IS:<name>` Dbxref or `product` containing "transposase") whose coordinates fall between two fragments of a gene, or immediately upstream of a gene's start, reproduces exactly the ramR/acrR/mgrB/ompK36 disruption signal. The ISKpn18-in-ramR and IS5-in-acrR cases are detectable this way; the I141T/A19V missense cases that **have no effects on overexpression of RamA** ([Spectrum](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.03204-24)) are correctly *not* flagged — the feature has built-in specificity.
- **The L3 loop GD insertion requires FAA, not GFF3.** It is a two-residue insertion; coordinates shift by 6 bp, which is within normal length variation. It needs alignment of the OmpK36 protein to a reference around residues 110–120. Given its documented **16-fold meropenem MIC increase with KPC-2** ([Nat Commun 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-11756-y)) and ST258 prevalence — and that ST258 is your largest ST at 612 isolates — this is simultaneously the highest-value FAA-derived feature and the one most entangled with lineage. It will need careful deconfounding: within-ST258 variation in L3 genotype is the signal to exploit.
- **Contig length (available in your GFF3 `##sequence-region`, and already parsed by the local code as `contig_length`) is a usable plasmid/chromosome proxy** — short contigs carrying AMR genes plus replicon/transposase annotations are likely plasmid-borne. This is imperfect and I found no source validating a specific length threshold, so treat it as a weak heuristic feature, not a determination.
- **Epistasis terms worth encoding explicitly** (each backed by a cited interaction above): carbapenemase × porin-loss; ESBL/AmpC × porin-loss; nfsA-LOF × nfsB-LOF; ramR-disruption × acrR-disruption; QRDR-mutation × PMQR-presence. These are the terms an EBM's pairwise interactions or an SCM conjunction can represent and a plain additive model cannot.

### Gaps
- **I could not verify Bakta's actual GFF3 attribute schema** — the readthedocs URL 404'd and the README omits it. The `IS:<name>` Dbxref convention, pseudogene flag representation, and whether `UniRef90` appears in GFF3 `Dbxref` (vs. only in TSV output) all need confirmation against a real output file.
- **No source quantified the sensitivity/specificity of detecting porin truncation from short-read assembly annotation** versus a reference method. Assembly fragmentation across repeated IS elements is a known hazard and will cause false "truncations."

## Q4. How recent papers validate putative novel determinants

### Takeaway
The accepted standard in high-profile venues is a **tiered** one: statistical discovery under explicit population-structure correction, then **replication in a phylogenetically independent cohort**, then **transposon-mutant or knockout plus in-trans complementation with MIC readout**. Crucially, published examples show candidates **failing** at the replication stage, which is what makes the standard meaningful.

### Cited findings
- **Conditional association to suppress lineage false positives, then experimental confirmation:** in *N. gonorrhoeae*, **conditioning on known 23S rRNA resistance mutations reduced false positives compared with single-locus GWAS and identified a G70D mutation in the RplD 50S ribosomal protein L4 as significantly associated with increased azithromycin resistance**; **the authors experimentally confirmed their GWAS results and found RplD G70D and other macrolide binding site mutations in 5.42% of 4,850 isolates** — [Nat Commun 2020, 10.1038/s41467-020-19250-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19250-6); [PMC7584619](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7584619/)
- **Independent-cohort replication, including a documented failure:** *M. tuberculosis* quantitative-phenotype GWAS tested associations in **1,452 isolates** and validated in **an independent data set of 792 patient isolates**, confirming **13 non-canonical loci**; notably, **prior knockout work had linked mymA deletion mutants to ethionamide resistance, but mymA could not be validated in the independent data** — [Nat Commun 2019, 10.1038/s41467-019-10110-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10110-6). **This is the single most instructive precedent: prior wet-lab evidence did not survive independent-cohort replication.**
- **Mutant-library validation and a methodological pivot:** in *S. aureus* bacteremia, a gene-burden GWAS produced **9 genes that reached genome-wide significance**, and the authors **used the Nebraska Transposon Mutant Library to validate 5 of the 9 genes**; they also reported that **variant-level GWAS was not sufficiently accurate to identify new targets**, motivating the gene-level aggregation — [ScienceDirect S221112472301080X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S221112472301080X)
- **Knockout + complementation as the standard causal design:** in an *E. coli* Keio collection screen, **mutants with increased susceptibility were identified and then validated via complementation assays** — [PMC12457515](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12457515/)
- **Reviewer-level expectation:** a bacterial GWAS review states that **integrating GWAS findings with laboratory validation also helps reduce false positives inherent in association studies** — [Appl Environ Microbiol 10.1128/aem.02512-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aem.02512-24); systematic review of GWAS approaches for AMR variants — [Microorganisms 10.3390/microorganisms11122866](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms11122866)
- **Held-out-clade as the ML-side evidence bar:** the PLOS Biology authors **propose testing on held-out clades to demonstrate generalizability** — [PMC12707637](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707637/)
- **Reference-genome / known-determinant cross-check** as a minimum bar for feature-importance claims: top SHAP SNPs were **placed on the K-12 MG1655 reference genome as a biological check** — [arXiv:2509.23552](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23552)
- **Structure-function analysis** as an additional evidence tier for novel determinants — [PubMed 42382359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42382359/)
- **Scale of the unvalidated-candidate problem:** one study of **over 70,000 genomes** found **over 5,000 sequences lacked homology to known genes, suggesting novel AMR-associated genes**, and a K. pneumoniae carbapenem GWAS **identified 28 and 37 potential marker genes associated with imipenem and meropenem resistance, respectively** — reported via [AEM review 10.1128/aem.02512-24](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aem.02512-24). These remain candidates, not determinants.
- Methodological alternative for representing determinants without presence/absence collapse: compacted de Bruijn graph GWAS (DBGWAS) — [PLOS Genet 10.1371/journal.pgen.1007758](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pgen.1007758); [bioRxiv 113563](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/113563v1.full)

### Inferences
- A defensible, wet-lab-free evidence ladder for your project, assembled from the above precedents: **(1)** feature survives lineage-blocked CV (PLOS Biology standard); **(2)** feature's association survives conditioning on known determinants for that drug (N. gonorrhoeae standard — e.g. does OmpK36 status still predict meropenem *after* conditioning on blaKPC presence?); **(3)** feature ranks stably across per-lineage refits and after top-feature removal (J Infect stability critique); **(4)** feature replicates in a held-out ST absent from training (M. tuberculosis standard); **(5)** feature is absent from or consistent with CARD/AMRFinderPlus. Reaching tier 4 without wet lab is what the M. tuberculosis paper achieved and is a realistic ceiling for your study.
- The honest framing for anything surviving tiers 1–5 is "candidate determinant requiring functional validation" — the M. tuberculosis *mymA* result demonstrates that even prior knockout evidence does not guarantee replication, so the reverse claim (replication implies causation) is also not safe.
- Conditioning on known determinants (tier 2) is the most under-used and highest-value step for your specific problem, because it directly separates "OmpK36 truncation predicts carbapenem resistance" from "OmpK36 truncation marks ST258, which carries blaKPC."

### Gaps
- I found **no single study** combining all four of ML/GWAS discovery, knockout, complementation, and held-out-lineage replication. The standard is assembled across papers, not exemplified by one.
- **No reviewer-stated explicit threshold** (e.g. "two independent cohorts required") was found; the evidence bar is inferred from what published papers did, not from stated editorial policy.
- I did not find a K. pneumoniae-specific example of a novel ML-discovered determinant that was subsequently functionally validated.

### Three things I'd flag for the design decision
1. **The strongest single lever** is not the model class — it's adding the lineage-blocked evaluation arm and reporting the random-vs-ST-blocked gap, because that gap is the quantity the PLOS Biology paper shows is large and that your 84–92% overlap makes unavoidable.
2. **The highest-value new feature families**, ranked by evidence strength × fraction of phenotypes a presence/absence model currently misses: (a) AMR gene **copy number** from duplicate CDS counts; (b) **IS-interruption** flags for ramR/acrR/ompK35/ompK36; (c) **CDS/protein length truncation** flags for porins and nitroreductases; (d) FAA-derived **QRDR residues** and **OmpK36 L3**.
3. **The most likely confound in your own results**: OmpK36 L3 GD insertion and blaKPC are both ST258-enriched, and ST258 is 612/3,949 of your isolates. Any importance attributed to either must be demonstrated *within* ST258 as well as across lineages, or it is indistinguishable from an ST258 indicator.
